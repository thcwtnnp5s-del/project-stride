## 2. A2 rename — "Rune-Worn Adventure" → "Waymarked"

**Owner instruction, recorded here. Not an ADR.**

A2 is renamed **Waymarked**. All A2 references in this document and in every
document from this point forward use that name.

**Why.** *Rune* carries a magical reading the direction is explicitly forbidden
to have under RA-1, and it is the single word most likely to make an image
generator produce glowing fantasy. The identity is practical route marks,
wayfinding marks, settlement marks, craft and resource symbols, and old but
unexplained iconography. `DIRECTION_A_IDENTITY_EXPLORATION_01.md` §A2.0 already
flagged the name as misleading under RA-1 and proposed *Waymarked* as a
replacement; the owner has taken that option.

**Treatment of the historical record.**

- `DIRECTION_A_IDENTITY_EXPLORATION_01.md` **keeps its A2 heading and body text
  as written.** It is the record of what was explored and approved under that
  name, and rewriting it to erase the working name would make the record less
  accurate rather than more.
- A **single-line pointer** is added to the existing *Name note* paragraph in
  that document's §A2.0, recording that the owner subsequently chose
  *Waymarked*, so a future reader does not treat the stale name as current. That
  is the only change made to it. Nothing else in that document is edited.
- The rename is **cosmetic**. It changes no constraint, no content, and no part
  of RA-1. A2 is the same direction with an accurate name.

**Unchanged by the rename.** A2 is **not eliminated and not ranked**. It receives
the same craft, the same specification depth, and the same production effort as
A1 and A3 in this round. Weakening a candidate to sharpen a contrast breaks the
experiment rather than resolving it
(`VISUAL_SAMPLE_GENERATION_01.md` §2; `DIRECTION_A_IDENTITY_EXPLORATION_01.md`
— *Current interpretation*).

### The A2 constraint, restated because it governs the prompt

Waymarked symbols remain **inert · non-glowing · non-magical · practical in
presentation · unexplained by new lore · compatible with existing world canon.**

Not permitted, in the prompt or in the sample: rune magic · ancient
civilizations · forgotten empires · mystical religions · glowing glyph systems ·
magical monuments · arcane script · invented alphabets · any implication of who
made the marks, when, or why.

Permitted: **wear as material observation.** This stone is more weathered than
that timber. This mark has been re-cut. Wear is visible fact; history is lore.

> **If Waymarked is distinctive only when a viewer imagines a backstory that does
> not exist, that is a weakness of the direction — and revealing it is what this
> comparison is for.** A2 is not to be propped up (RA-1).

## 3. The common rendering floor — identical in A1, A2, and A3

All three are **inside Classic Pixel MMO Lite**. They are three identity
treatments of one rendering philosophy, **not three art styles**.

Every sample must preserve:

- obvious, intentional pixel-art identity
- restrained rendering — construction over effect
- grounded fantasy
- baked, simple, single-direction light
- **no** cinematic lighting · **no** bloom · **no** light shafts
- **no** premium material rendering
- **no** heavy atmospheric haze
- **no** constant particle layer
- economical environmental detail
- quiet terrain beneath gameplay-important objects
- strong, readable silhouettes
- mobile readability, judged at phone size in a hand
- low animation burden implied by the still frame
- practical asset scalability
- WalkScape-compatible world presence
- **no** joystick, free-roam, or player-steered-locomotion implication (R-4)

### The anti-drift rule, stated as an enforceable line

> **No identity may win by being more richly rendered.**

If a sample looks better because it received more light, more material, more
haze, more particles, or more detail density than the other two, the experiment
has been broken rather than won. Distinctiveness comes from **design language** —
shape grammar, motif, silhouette, palette discipline, icon geometry, node
dressing, architectural grammar, and implied animation personality
(`VISUAL_SAMPLE_GENERATION_01.md` §14.1 points 3 and 4).

The test to apply to any accepted sample: *is this better because it was
**rendered** more, or because it was **designed** better?* If the honest answer is
"rendered more", reject and re-roll.

## 7. SHARED DIRECTION A FLOOR BLOCK — identical text in A1, A2, and A3

> *New for this round. In `VISUAL_SAMPLE_GENERATION_01.md` these properties lived
> inside Direction A's own block, because A was one candidate among three. Here
> all three samples are Direction A, so the floor is shared text and only
> identity differs. Any of it appearing in an identity block instead would let
> one treatment intensify it.*

> Art direction: classic pixel art, in the register of a traditional 2D pixel MMO
> — familiar, grounded, honest about being a sprite game, and deliberately
> restrained but carefully crafted. Restraint here is a craft choice, not an
> absence of craft: every element is finished and deliberate, never sketchy,
> unfinished, or crude.
>
> Low-resolution pixel construction with visible, deliberate pixels and hard
> pixel edges throughout; crisp nearest-neighbour scaling, no anti-aliasing, no
> blur, no soft gradients, no sub-pixel smoothing. Every element is built on the
> same consistent pixel grid at the same pixel density — the character, the
> world, and the interface are all made of the same size of pixel.
>
> A restrained palette of roughly forty to fifty colours in total. Flat
> three-step colour ramps with no dithering. Selective one-pixel dark outlines on
> characters, resource nodes, and other interactable objects; terrain and
> background are unoutlined, so the outline itself signals what can be touched.
>
> A single fixed light direction from the upper left, baked flatly into every
> element. No dynamic lighting, no bloom, no rim light, no specular highlights,
> no glow, no emission, no atmospheric haze, no light shafts, no particles, no
> weather effects. Depth is carried purely by value and saturation — the
> background loses contrast and takes a slight cool tint, the foreground keeps
> full saturation. A soft elliptical contact shadow sits under each standing
> object; there are no cast directional shadows.
>
> The character is compact and sturdy, roughly a 1:4.5 head-to-body ratio, with a
> clear readable silhouette and a visible gap between arm and torso. The face is
> minimal: two eye pixels, no nose, expression carried by head tilt and posture
> rather than by features. Equipment reads by silhouette first and colour second
> — the sword is identifiable from its outline alone.
>
> Terrain is tile-based with a deliberately small vocabulary, with variation
> coming from a few scattered overlays rather than from many tiles. The ground
> layer is quiet on purpose, so the character silhouette and the gatherable plant
> never get lost in it; ground detail is suppressed within and immediately around
> the plant node's footprint. Buildings are built from a small modular kit.
> Trees are clustered value-blob canopies of three value steps with a visible
> trunk, not individual leaves. Environmental detail is economical throughout.
>
> The gatherable plant sits one step higher in saturation and one step higher in
> contrast than any other green in the frame. Its separation from the ground is
> achieved by palette and silhouette alone — never by glow, bloom, emission, or
> any light effect.
>
> The interface is seated flush against the top and bottom edges of the frame in
> solid opaque panels, with chunky pixel icons for the six tabs and a small
> anchored opaque plate for the gather card. Banked walking energy is presented
> as a stock the player owns — a numeral with a boot glyph — never as a draining
> meter, a countdown, or a refill prompt.
>
> Charming, calm, warm, and legible at arm's length in one glance. Nothing on
> screen competes for attention.

**Where the floor deliberately stays silent:** UI *frame geometry*, icon
*geometry*, signpost *treatment*, architectural *grammar*, motif language,
palette *family*, node *dressing*, and character *bearing*. Those are the signal,
and each identity block supplies them.

## 7.1 IDENTITY BLOCK — A1 · FRONTIER HEARTH

**Prompt thesis.** *A frontier holding that has been maintained.* A1's
distinctiveness comes from **visible repair, maintenance, and practical
construction** — braced timber, exposed joins and fasteners, newer wood patched
into older wood, a UI made of fitted planks and corner brackets. Everything
visible has a job. Warmth comes from **palette and object choice**, never from
light.

**Append to §6 + §7, then append §8.**

> Identity treatment: a maintained frontier holding. The governing shape language
> is the sturdy rectangle with a working joint — forms are squared, braced, and
> visibly fastened, and where two things meet, the fastening is the detail.
>
> The settlement is built and repaired rather than merely old. The palisade gate
> is a braced timber gate between squared posts with a heavy lintel and an
> obvious diagonal cross-brace, and one section of the palisade is a visible
> repair in newer, lighter timber against the weathered grey-brown of the rest.
> The repair is the identity and must be clearly readable. Wall spans show corner
> posts and plank seams, and pegs or nail heads are visible at the joints as
> small deliberate pixel marks. Buildings are low and wide with steep simple roof
> pitches in muted clay-brown, never taller than they are wide. Stone appears
> only as foundations and thresholds, distinguished from timber by value rather
> than by texture.
>
> The signpost that is already in the scene is a squared timber post whose two
> arms are carved planks, each fixed with a visible peg; direction is shown by
> the arms' angles. The firewood
> stack is squarely stacked, split logs with flat cut faces, the work of someone
> tidy. The scrub along the palisade is sparse and useful. The path shows visible
> tread compaction at the gate mouth and stays quiet elsewhere.
>
> The player character reads as a competent traveller with good boots and a full
> pack rather than as a chosen hero: broad-shouldered, wide-stanced, bulk sitting
> at the shoulders and the boots, the canvas pack squaring the upper back and
> breaking the shoulder line while the sword breaks the hip line on the opposite
> side. The gear looks issued and used — practical layers, an honest belt, wrapped
> boots — the kit a settlement hands a newcomer because it works. The resident by
> the gate reads as occupied: plain layered clothing with sleeves pushed back.
>
> The gatherable plant is a dense, grounded, flat-based tuft that sits on the
> earth rather than emerging from it, and the ground immediately around it is
> trodden, as though this patch is regularly visited.
>
> The interface chrome is plank-and-bracket: flat opaque panels reading as fitted
> timber, with small corner brackets and a visible seam line along each bar. The
> bevel is a construction line, not a shine. The six tab icons are squared-off
> with one rounded working edge and read as tools and goods — a bundle, a billet,
> a haft, a pot. The active tab plate is raised, lit from the same baked upper-left
> direction as the world. The gather card is a small framed opaque plate,
> bracketed at its corners.
>
> Palette family: honest earth — weathered grey-brown timber, muted clay roofs,
> warm oat and sand cloth, dusty path browns, muted grass greens, with cool
> grey-stone and slate as the counterweight so the frame does not sit entirely in
> the warm half of the wheel.
>
> Emotional register: calm competence. The satisfaction of a sharpened tool, a
> stacked woodpile, a mended wall. Warm without being soft. Lived-in without being
> cluttered. Workmanlike rather than decorative.

**A1 negatives — append to §8 for A1 only:**

> Not cottagecore, not a cozy farming game, not a village life simulator. No
> pastel timber, no rounded storybook roofs, no window boxes, no bunting, no
> hearts, no cute softness. No decorative or luxury objects, no ornament that
> nobody uses, no clutter or extra furniture of any kind, and no object added to
the scene for any reason — no tools, no workbench, no chimney or stovepipe, no
extra logs, no additional timber. No cinematic warmth, no
> golden glow, no firelight bloom. No apron, no market presentation, no smiling
> shopkeeper. The frame must not be entirely brown — keep cool grey-stone and
> green present.

## 7.2 IDENTITY BLOCK — A2 · WAYMARKED

**Prompt thesis.** *A marked world, and the marks still work.* A2's
distinctiveness comes from **one practical symbol vocabulary repeated across
world and UI** — the same drawn mark family on the signpost's arms, the
gateposts, one of the two path stones, a resident's belt plate, the interface
chrome, the six tab icons, and the gather card's herb icon. The marks are **functional, inert, and
unexplained.**

**Carriers are existing scene elements only (RS-1).** The earlier proposal placed
a new waystone at the signpost's position and a new companion marker stone beside
the Meadow Patch. **Both are withdrawn** — they were additional physical objects,
and A2 does not need them: the thesis is *one vocabulary repeated*, and repetition

## 11. Exact external generation workflow

Claude Code cannot render these samples. Verified against this session's actual
tool surface, not assumed: file, search, shell, browser-automation, scheduling,
subagent, and document-publishing tools are present, and **no text-to-image model
and no connected image-generation MCP server**.

Two things this environment *can* draw — hand-authored SVG and HTML/CSS mockups —
are deliberately **not** offered as samples. Either would be Claude drawing the
picture rather than an identity being tested.

### 11.1 Division of responsibility

| Owned by Claude Code | Owned by the image-generation environment |
|---|---|
| The controlled scene and its constraints | Final text-to-image execution |
| The reused control image | Model, sampler, and conditioning-strength choice |
| The three identity specifications | Re-rolls until §9 passes |
| The three prompts and the shared blocks | — |
| The validity and failure checklists | — |

### 11.2 The run, step by step

1. **Choose one tool and one model version, and do not change either mid-round.**
   Any text-to-image service that accepts **a reference image plus a long prompt**
   at a fixed portrait aspect ratio. The assembled prompts run roughly 1,100–1,400
   words; a service that silently truncates or rewrites long prompts will drop the
   shared scene block and the comparison will fail *quietly* rather than loudly.
   Verify the full prompt was received before running the round.
2. **Supply `composition_blockout.png` as the structural reference to all three.**
   Same file, same conditioning mode, same strength.
3. **Fix the seed.** One seed for the round. Same seed for A1, A2, and A3.
4. **Fix the size** at 1024 × 1536.
5. **Assemble each prompt** in the §5 order: shared scene block (§6) → shared
   floor block (§7) → identity block (§7.1 / §7.2 / §7.3) → shared negative block
   (§8) → that identity's own negatives. **Copy the shared blocks; do not retype
   them.**
6. **Tune conditioning strength on A1 first, before running the round.** Strong
   enough to hold placement, weak enough that the blockout's flat masses do not
   leak into the rendering as blocky geometry. *If the output starts looking like
   the blockout, the conditioning is too strong* (V-12). Once tuned, freeze it and
   use the same value for all three.
7. **Run all three in one session,** back to back, with nothing changed between
   them.
8. **Re-roll on validity failure, not on taste.** Run §9.1 and §9.2 on each
   sample. Re-roll any sample that fails, changing nothing but the roll. Take the
   **first valid** sample per identity — not the prettiest of several valid ones,
   which would reintroduce the judgement the controls exist to remove.
9. **Run §9.3 across the three together** before any of them is looked at as art.
10. **Then score identity** using §10 and the owner's own reaction. The reaction
    matters more than any checklist; the checklists exist to make it articulable.

### 11.3 Two failure modes worth naming before the run

- **A2 glow leakage.** Even without the word *runes*, generators associate carved
  stone symbols with fantasy magic. If A2 returns glowing marks repeatedly, that
  is a *prompt-and-model* problem, not evidence about the direction — re-roll,
  and if it persists, strengthen the A2 negative block rather than accepting a
  glowing sample or weakening the identity.
- **A3 path erasure.** Tread compaction and a braid are subtle, and generators
  smooth them away. If the path comes back as a plain brown strip, A3-F7 has
  fired and the sample did not test A3.
- **Object invention.** Generators add furniture to fill space, and both A2 and
  A3 describe things — marks, trails — that a model associates with markers,
  cairns, and posts. Run V-3a by **counting**, not by impression, on every roll.
  An invented object is a re-roll, never a sample that is "close enough".

### 11.4 What the round does not test

Living Activity Presentation · animation personality in motion · activity poses ·
combat · regional variation · Direction A's production pipeline. All are
deliberately outside Round 1 (RA-2), and **no Round 1 result is evidence about
any of them.**
